The Stare Test · Experiment 01 — Afterimage

Stare at it.Then look away.

Twenty seconds with your eyes locked on the star. Then look at a white wall, a ceiling, the blank screen we leave you — the two faces come with you. That's not a trick. That's your eyes, and it's the whole idea behind this collection.

See them?

Two faces, floating on nothing. Your retina got tired of the reds and blues and served you the opposite — a negative burned into your vision for a few seconds. We liked that feeling enough to build a whole collection on it. This exact artwork is printed on the back of the AFTERIMAGE tee.

Why it works

Stare at anything bright long enough and the color receptors in your eyes start to fatigue — they stop firing at full strength. Look at a blank surface and your brain gets an unbalanced signal, so it paints in the complementary colors. The image flips. The faces you saw were blue and red; the ones following you now lean warm and green. Same picture, opposite charge.

Bridget Riley was doing this to gallery walls in the 1960s. Julio Le Parc too. We're doing it on a tee in New York, because a print you can wear should do something to the people looking at it.

Didn't see it? Get closer to the screen, raise the brightness, and give it the full 30 seconds. It works on almost everyone — the ones it fails on usually moved their eyes.
